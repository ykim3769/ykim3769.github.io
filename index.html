Research Focus Areas

Five connected directions, from how people perceive the visual world to machines that see, generate and act.

Computational Vision and Visual Perception

Human perception of transparency, occlusion, and lightness (doctoral and postdoctoral work at Cambridge, Utrecht, and Smith-Kettlewell); current psychophysical studies on occluding vs. shadow/spotlight transparency and on anorthoscopic (slit-viewing) perception, using calibrated displays and eye tracking.

17 papers →

Multimodal Perception and Human-Centered Computing

Gaze, gesture, facial-expression, and affect modeling; wearable human–drone interfaces; and UI/UX design for generative AI.

22 papers →

Human-Centered Sensing for Health and Safety

Driver drowsiness and fatigue detection, fake-emotion and affective-state recognition, 3D gaze estimation from natural face images, and video anomaly detection for human and infrastructure safety.

23 papers →

Generative and Multimodal AI

U-Net and U-Net Encapsulated Transformer (UET) architectures for generative models and LLMs; language grounding for 3D navigation; instruction-driven facial animation; sign-language production; and LLM–RL coupling for interactive 3D scene generation.

8 papers →

Visual Learning for Autonomous Systems

Reinforcement learning and visual representation learning for UAV navigation and obstacle avoidance; simulation-based training; and event-camera/RGB fusion for low-light perception.
